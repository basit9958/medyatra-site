Tata Memorial Centre in Mumbai's Parel district is India's oldest and most academically prominent cancer hospital, in continuous operation since 1941 and an autonomous institution under the Department of Atomic Energy. It functions as a comprehensive cancer centre — surgery, medical oncology, radiation oncology, paediatric oncology and bone marrow transplant — and as a research and training institution.

The clinical programmes handle very high case volumes across most solid tumour types and haematological malignancies. The paediatric oncology programme is one of the largest in the country. The hospital operates a tiered service model that combines extensive subsidised care for Indian patients with private-tariff pathways that international patients can access for elective tertiary care.

International patient services are coordinated through a separate desk that arranges invitation letters, ground transport from Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj International Airport (around 21 km away), accommodation matching and interpretation. Arabic interpretation is available on request.

Accommodation for accompanying family is available in serviced apartments and partner hotels in Parel and adjacent neighbourhoods. Many patients in active treatment with frequent OPD visits choose accommodation within walking distance of the hospital entrance.
